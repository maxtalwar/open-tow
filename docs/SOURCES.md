# Sources and provenance

## Primary design source

Mark F. Cancian, Matthew Cancian, and Eric Heginbotham, *The First Battle of the Next War: Wargaming a Chinese Invasion of Taiwan*, Center for Strategic and International Studies, January 2023.

- Landing page: <https://www.csis.org/analysis/first-battle-next-war-wargaming-chinese-invasion-taiwan>
- Public report: <https://csis-website-prod.s3.amazonaws.com/s3fs-public/publication/230109_Cancian_FirstBattle_NextWar.pdf>

The report is the basis for the scenario's time scale, broad map abstraction, aggregate unit treatment, mission categories, sequencing, amphibious-lift concept, sustainment constraint, finite munitions, and iteration methodology.

## Professional-wargaming quality guidance

- U.S. Government Accountability Office, *Defense Analysis: Additional Actions Could Enhance DOD's Wargaming Efforts*, GAO-23-105351: <https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-23-105351>
- Center for Army Lessons Learned, *How to Master Wargaming*: <https://api.army.mil/e2/c/downloads/2023/01/31/bf65892d/20-06-how-to-master-wargaming-public.pdf>

These references inform the emphasis on explicit objectives, consistent adjudication, human review, data collection, reproducibility, and limitations.

## Not imported

- No CSIS art, map images, card files, or unpublished umpire materials.
- No classified or controlled information.
- No operational plans or real-time feeds.
- No proprietary commercial-game mechanics or assets.

## Gaps to resolve

An exact reproduction would require permission and access to the full CSIS rules for umpires, backup order-of-battle papers, and supporting calculations. Until then, the project should be described as a reconstruction inspired by the published game—not a rules-identical port.
